1. Load Weight

Before planning a lift, the load weight should be established from reliable information such as drawings, manufacturer data, verified records or approved calculations.

Weight = Volume × Material Density

2. Center of Gravity

The center of gravity is the point where the load's weight can be considered to act. Understanding its location is important when selecting lifting points and rigging arrangements.

3. Sling Angle

Sling angle affects the tension in the sling legs. As the angle becomes smaller from the horizontal, sling tension increases.

Sling leg force = Load component ÷ appropriate angle factor

Always use the applicable standard, manufacturer's information and approved engineering calculations for actual lifting work.

4. WLL / SWL

WLL means Working Load Limit. SWL means Safe Working Load. Always verify the marked capacity and configuration of the actual lifting equipment being used.

5. Load Distribution

Loads supported by multiple sling legs or lifting points may not be shared equally. The actual load distribution depends on the geometry, center of gravity, connection points and rigging arrangement.

6. Crane Capacity

Crane capacity depends on factors such as crane configuration, operating radius, boom length, counterweight and ground conditions. The manufacturer's current load chart must be used for actual work.

7. Ground Bearing Pressure

Crane support loads must be considered together with the capacity of the supporting ground and any mats or support systems. Ground conditions should be verified by the responsible competent personnel.

8. Example Study Question

A load has a verified weight of 10 tonnes. Before selecting lifting equipment, what information should be checked?

Study answer: Load weight, center of gravity, lifting points, rigging configuration, equipment WLL, sling angles, crane capacity, radius, ground conditions and the approved lifting plan.

Important Safety Notice

These examples are for education only. They must not be used as the basis for an actual lift. Real lifting calculations must use verified load information, applicable standards, manufacturer data, approved engineering calculations and competent-person review.
