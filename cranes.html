Crane Technical Details

Truck-Mounted Crane

Mobile crane

A truck-mounted crane lifts and places loads at multiple locations. Actual capacity depends on boom configuration, radius, counterweight, stabilizer setup and the manufacturer's load chart.

Key Crane Parts

  1. Truck chassis and carrier
  2. Boom base and telescopic boom sections
  3. Slewing unit and turntable
  4. Hoist winch, drum and wire rope
  5. Hook block, hook and safety latch
  6. Hydraulic pump, cylinders and hoses
  7. Outriggers, stabilizers and pads
  8. Counterweight, where fitted
  9. Operator cab and controls
  10. Load moment indicator, where fitted

Related Devices / Accessories

  • Wire rope and reeving components
  • Slings: chain, wire rope or textile
  • Shackles and connectors
  • Spreader or lifting beam, when required
  • Outrigger mats or load-spreading pads
  • Remote control, where fitted
  • Tag lines where appropriate to the lift plan

Pre-use Safety Inspection

  1. Inspect hook, latch, wire rope and visible terminations.
  2. Check boom sections, pins, retainers and structural condition.
  3. Inspect hydraulic hoses and cylinders for leaks or damage.
  4. Check stabilizers, pads, locking devices and ground conditions.
  5. Check brakes, controls, alarms and safety devices.
  6. Verify the load chart matches the actual configuration.
  7. Confirm load weight, centre of gravity and lifting points.
  8. Check accessories for identification, damage and suitability.

Safety Information

  • Use suitable ground and the manufacturer's setup procedure.
  • Keep people clear of outriggers and the suspended load.
  • Maintain required clearance from overhead power lines.
  • Use an agreed signal system and exclusion zone.
  • Never guess load capacity or use the wrong load chart.

Main Hazards

  • Overturning due to poor ground or incorrect setup
  • Overloading at extended radius
  • Electrical-line contact
  • Crushing near outriggers or rotating structure
  • Load swing or dropped loads

Crawler Crane

Mobile crane

A crawler crane travels on tracks and is used for heavy construction and industrial lifting.

Key Parts

  1. Crawler tracks and track frames
  2. Upper and lower carriages
  3. Boom sections, pins and retainers
  4. Boom hoist system
  5. Main and auxiliary hoists
  6. Wire ropes, drums and sheaves
  7. Hook blocks and counterweights
  8. Slewing ring, drives and controls

Related Devices

  • Main and auxiliary hooks
  • Slings and shackles
  • Lifting beams
  • Load monitoring devices
  • Ground mats or engineered platform
  • Travel alarms and communication devices

Safety Inspection

  1. Inspect tracks, rollers and idlers.
  2. Check boom members, pins and retainers.
  3. Inspect hoist ropes, sheaves and hook blocks.
  4. Verify counterweight configuration.
  5. Check brakes, controls and alarms.
  6. Assess ground bearing capacity for lifting and travel.
  7. Confirm boom configuration and radius against the load chart.

Safety and Hazards

  • Ground failure under tracks
  • Incorrect boom or counterweight configuration
  • Load swing and collision
  • High winds
  • Follow approved assembly and travel procedures; keep people clear of moving tracks and loads.

Tower Crane

Fixed crane

A tower crane lifts materials at construction sites using a mast, jib, trolley and hoist.

Key Parts

  1. Foundation and anchoring system
  2. Mast sections and connections
  3. Slewing ring and drive
  4. Working jib and counter-jib
  5. Counterweights
  6. Trolley and hoist
  7. Wire rope and hook block
  8. Climbing system, where fitted
  9. Limit and monitoring devices

Related Devices

  • Hooks and lifting blocks
  • Slings and shackles
  • Wind-speed monitoring
  • Load and moment monitoring
  • Anti-collision system, where fitted
  • Trolley and hoist limit switches

Safety Inspection

  1. Review erection, foundation and tie records.
  2. Inspect mast joints, bolts and pins.
  3. Check ropes, sheaves, hook block and rigging.
  4. Test brakes, limits and protective systems as required.
  5. Check wind monitoring and operating limits.
  6. Review anti-collision arrangements where installed.

Safety and Hazards

  • High winds and gusts
  • Collision with structures or other cranes
  • Falling objects and load swing
  • Unauthorized erection or adjustments
  • Only authorized personnel should erect, climb or dismantle the crane.

Rough-Terrain Crane

Mobile crane

A rough-terrain crane is designed for lifting at construction sites with challenging ground conditions.

Key Parts

  1. Chassis, tires and axles
  2. Steering and travel system
  3. Telescopic boom
  4. Slewing platform
  5. Hydraulic system
  6. Outriggers and stabilizers
  7. Hoist, rope and hook block
  8. Controls and safety monitoring

Related Devices

  • Hooks, slings and shackles
  • Outrigger pads and mats
  • Level indicator
  • Capacity indicator, where fitted
  • Communication equipment
  • Warning alarms and lights

Safety Inspection

  1. Check tires, wheels, steering and brakes.
  2. Inspect boom, pins, hoses and cylinders.
  3. Inspect hook block and accessories.
  4. Check stabilizers, pads and level.
  5. Verify load chart for actual configuration.
  6. Assess slope, ground bearing and obstructions.

Safety and Hazards

  • Weak or sloping ground
  • Overturning and hydraulic failure
  • Blind spots and overhead obstructions
  • Use approved setup configurations.
  • Do not lift on a slope unless permitted by the manufacturer.

Overhead Bridge Crane

Industrial crane

An overhead crane travels along elevated runway rails in factories, workshops and warehouses.

Key Parts

  1. Bridge girders
  2. End trucks and wheels
  3. Runway rails and supports
  4. Trolley frame
  5. Hoist motor and gearbox
  6. Wire rope or load chain
  7. Hook block
  8. Brakes, controls and limit devices

Related Devices

  • Wire-rope or chain hoist
  • Hooks and latches
  • Slings and shackles
  • Pendant or radio remote
  • Travel limit switches
  • Warning alarms and overload protection, where fitted

Safety Inspection

  1. Inspect hook, latch, rope or chain.
  2. Check hoist and travel brakes.
  3. Check end stops, limits and warning devices.
  4. Inspect visible wheels, rails and structure.
  5. Test controls and emergency stop as instructed.
  6. Report unusual noise, drift, vibration or overheating.

Safety and Hazards

  • Crushing between loads and fixed objects
  • Overloading or side pulling
  • Dropped loads and unexpected movement
  • Never allow anyone under a suspended load.
  • Isolate and report defective equipment.

Gantry Crane

Industrial crane

A gantry crane is supported by legs and moves on wheels or rails, often in ports and outdoor yards.

Key Parts

  1. Main girder
  2. Supporting legs
  3. End carriages and wheels
  4. Rail or travel system
  5. Trolley and hoist
  6. Wire rope or chain
  7. Hook block
  8. Brakes, drives and controls

Related Devices

  • Hooks, slings and lifting beams
  • Travel alarms and lights
  • Limit switches
  • Rail clamps or storm restraints, where fitted
  • Remote control and load monitoring

Safety Inspection

  1. Inspect girders, legs, connections and visible welds.
  2. Check rails, wheels, drives and end stops.
  3. Inspect hoist, rope or chain, hook and brakes.
  4. Verify travel limits and warning devices.
  5. Check the travel path and weather restrictions.

Safety and Hazards

  • Crushing during travel
  • Rail or wheel defects
  • Wind exposure and load swing
  • Keep people clear of moving legs.
  • Follow applicable wind and storm-securing procedures.

Jib Crane

Fixed crane

A jib crane performs localized lifting around a workstation, machine shop or assembly area.

Key Parts

  1. Mast or wall bracket
  2. Jib arm
  3. Trolley, where fitted
  4. Hoist and drive
  5. Slewing bearing or pivot
  6. Anchors and foundation
  7. Hook, rope or chain
  8. Controls and limits

Related Devices

  • Chain or wire-rope hoist
  • Hook and latch
  • Slings and shackles
  • Trolley and slewing stops
  • Pendant or remote control
  • Mounting bolts and anchors

Safety Inspection

  1. Inspect jib arm, mast and visible welds.
  2. Check anchors and mounting bolts.
  3. Inspect hoist chain or rope, hook and latch.
  4. Check trolley, brakes and stops.
  5. Verify rated capacity and working radius.
  6. Report looseness or abnormal movement.

Safety and Hazards

  • Anchor or support failure
  • Overloading at outer radius
  • Pinch points during slewing
  • Do not modify anchors without engineering approval.
  • Keep people clear of the load swing path.

Floating Crane

Special-purpose crane

A floating crane is installed on a vessel or floating platform for marine and offshore lifting operations.

Key Parts

  1. Floating platform or vessel
  2. Crane pedestal and slewing system
  3. Boom and hoist
  4. Wire ropes and hook blocks
  5. Ballast and stability systems
  6. Mooring or positioning system
  7. Power and controls
  8. Environmental monitoring

Related Devices

  • Marine-suitable rigging
  • Hooks and lifting beams
  • Load monitoring instruments
  • Mooring and anchoring equipment
  • Communications and emergency equipment
  • Weather and sea-state monitoring

Safety Inspection

  1. Review vessel and crane inspection records.
  2. Inspect structure, ropes, hooks and rigging.
  3. Verify ballast, stability and mooring arrangements.
  4. Check vessel motion, weather and sea state.
  5. Confirm the lift plan accounts for dynamic loading.
  6. Check communications and stop-work criteria.

Safety and Hazards

  • Vessel heave, roll and pitch
  • Changing load dynamics
  • Adverse weather and sea state
  • Mooring or positioning failure
  • Use a marine-specific lift plan and environmental limits.

Articulating Boom Crane

Mobile crane

An articulating boom crane uses jointed hydraulic boom sections, commonly for truck loading, delivery and maintenance lifting.

Key Parts

  1. Vehicle chassis and mounting frame
  2. Articulated boom sections
  3. Hydraulic cylinders and hoses
  4. Slewing column
  5. Stabilizers and support pads
  6. Hook or lifting attachment
  7. Controls and emergency stop
  8. Capacity monitoring, where fitted

Related Devices

  • Approved hook or attachment
  • Slings and shackles
  • Hydraulic stabilizers
  • Remote control, where fitted
  • Outrigger mats where required
  • Load restraint equipment for transport

Safety Inspection

  1. Inspect boom sections, pins and retainers.
  2. Check hydraulic hoses, cylinders and connections.
  3. Inspect hook, latch and lifting accessories.
  4. Check stabilizers, mounting frame and pads.
  5. Test controls and emergency stop as specified.
  6. Verify the load chart for actual boom position.
  7. Confirm vehicle security and work-area clearance.

Safety and Hazards

  • Overturning due to incorrect stabilizer setup
  • Hydraulic hose failure
  • Pinch points at boom joints
  • Overloading at extended reach
  • Use the correct chart and keep hands clear of articulated joints.

General Lifting Safety — All Crane Types

  • Confirm load weight, centre of gravity and suitable lifting points.
  • Verify the crane configuration and rated capacity from the manufacturer's load chart.
  • Check accessories for suitability, identification, damage and required inspection status.
  • Inspect the work area, ground, weather, obstructions and electrical-line clearances.
  • Use competent operators, riggers and signal persons.
  • Establish an exclusion zone and agreed communication procedure.
  • Never stand beneath a suspended load or bypass safety devices.
  • Stop work if conditions become unsafe, the load behaves unexpectedly or communications fail.

Standards and Guidance

  • LEEA publications and guidance relevant to lifting equipment and accessories.
  • Relevant EN crane standards, such as EN 13000 where applicable to mobile cranes.
  • Manufacturer operating, inspection, maintenance and discard instructions.
  • Applicable national legislation, site lifting procedures and risk assessments.
  • TÜV inspection or certification requirements applicable to the equipment and jurisdiction.
Important: This is educational guidance, not an official TÜV or LEEA certification or a substitute for a competent-person examination. Applicable inspection intervals, discard criteria and legal requirements depend on equipment type, use, jurisdiction and current standards.
