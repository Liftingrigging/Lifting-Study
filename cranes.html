Crane Study Reference

This section covers the main crane types used in lifting operations, their basic components, classification, operating characteristics, inspection points and commonly referenced standards.

For actual lifting operations, always use the crane manufacturer's load chart, operating manual, current inspection requirements, approved lift plan and applicable Saudi/project requirements.

1. Mobile Cranes

Mobile cranes are cranes mounted on mobile carriers and are widely used for construction, maintenance, plant work and heavy lifting.

Types

  • Telescopic mobile crane
  • Truck-mounted crane
  • Rough-terrain crane
  • All-terrain crane
  • Crawler crane

Main Components

  • Carrier
  • Upper works
  • Boom
  • Counterweight
  • Hoist drum
  • Wire rope
  • Hook block
  • Outriggers where fitted
  • Load moment / rated capacity limiting system

Common Standard

ASME B30.5 covers mobile and locomotive cranes. It addresses construction, inspection, testing, maintenance and operation.

2. Crawler Cranes

Crawler cranes travel on crawler tracks and are commonly used for heavy lifting where high capacity and site mobility are required.

Types

  • Lattice boom crawler crane
  • Telescopic crawler crane
  • Heavy-lift crawler crane

Main Components

  • Crawler tracks
  • Upper works
  • Lattice or telescopic boom
  • Boom foot
  • Hoist system
  • Counterweights
  • Hook block
  • Load line

Crawler cranes are included within the scope of ASME B30.5 where they meet the standard's applicable characteristics.

3. Tower Cranes

Tower cranes are fixed or traveling-base cranes with a vertical tower and horizontal or luffing jib.

Types

  • Hammerhead tower crane
  • Flat-top tower crane
  • Luffing-jib tower crane
  • Self-erecting tower crane

Main Components

  • Tower / mast
  • Jib
  • Counter-jib
  • Counterweights
  • Trolley
  • Hoist mechanism
  • Hook block
  • Slewing mechanism
  • Climbing system where fitted

Standards

  • ASME B30.3 - Tower Cranes
  • ASME B30.29 - Self-Erecting Tower Cranes
  • SASO GSO ISO 4301-3 - Tower Crane Classification

4. Overhead & Gantry Cranes

Overhead and gantry cranes are commonly used in workshops, warehouses, fabrication facilities and industrial plants.

Types

  • Single-girder overhead crane
  • Double-girder overhead crane
  • Top-running crane
  • Under-running crane
  • Gantry crane
  • Semi-gantry crane
  • Monorail system

Main Components

  • Bridge girder
  • End trucks
  • Runway
  • Trolley
  • Hoist
  • Wire rope or chain
  • Hook
  • Controls

Standard

ASME B30.17 covers cranes and monorails with underhung trolley or bridge arrangements. ASME also lists B30.2 for top-running overhead and gantry cranes.

5. Jib Cranes

Jib cranes use a horizontal or near-horizontal jib to move loads within a defined working area.

Types

  • Free-standing jib crane
  • Wall-mounted jib crane
  • Column-mounted jib crane
  • Foundation-mounted jib crane
  • Traveling jib crane

Main Components

  • Jib
  • Column or support
  • Slew mechanism
  • Trolley
  • Hoist
  • Hook

Applicable classification and equipment requirements should be checked against the crane design and applicable standard.

6. Portal & Pedestal Cranes

Portal and pedestal cranes are commonly used in industrial, marine, port and material-handling applications.

Main Features

  • Fixed or traveling base
  • Slewing upper structure
  • Boom
  • Hoisting system
  • Luffing system

Standard

ASME B30.4 covers portal and pedestal cranes.

7. Articulating Boom Cranes

Articulating boom cranes use hydraulically articulated boom sections and are commonly mounted on mobile or stationary bases.

Types

  • Truck-mounted articulating crane
  • Loader crane
  • Stationary articulating crane
  • Trailer-mounted articulating crane

Standard

ASME B30.22 covers articulating boom cranes.

Important Checks

  • Boom configuration
  • Hydraulic system
  • Outrigger configuration
  • Load chart
  • Rated capacity limiter
  • Hook and lifting accessories

8. Truck-Mounted Cranes

Truck-mounted cranes combine a crane with a road-going vehicle carrier.

Common Types

  • Truck crane
  • Knuckle-boom truck crane
  • Loader crane
  • Service crane

Important Checks

  • Outrigger deployment
  • Ground condition
  • Crane level
  • Load chart
  • Radius
  • Boom configuration
  • Vehicle stability

9. Rough Terrain Cranes

Rough-terrain cranes are designed primarily for off-road operation on construction sites.

Characteristics

  • Four-wheel mobile carrier
  • Telescopic boom
  • Outriggers
  • Compact dimensions
  • Off-road capability

Applicable requirements for mobile cranes should be checked against the crane's design and governing standard.

10. All Terrain Cranes

All-terrain cranes combine road mobility with off-road capability and are commonly used for large construction and industrial lifts.

Main Features

  • Multiple axles
  • Telescopic boom
  • Multiple steering modes
  • Outriggers
  • Counterweights
  • Advanced load monitoring systems

Mobile-crane requirements and the manufacturer's load chart must govern the actual configuration.

11. Telehandlers Used for Lifting

Telehandlers can be used for lifting loads only when the machine, attachment, configuration and manufacturer instructions permit the lifting application.

Important Components

  • Telescopic boom
  • Fork carriage or approved lifting attachment
  • Hydraulic cylinders
  • Chassis
  • Stabilizers where fitted
  • Load chart / rated capacity system
Do not treat a telehandler as a crane simply because it can raise a load. The manufacturer's approved lifting configuration and applicable standard must permit the intended operation.

12. Main Crane Components

Component Function
Boom Supports and positions the load line and lifting equipment.
Jib Extends the crane's working reach where fitted.
Hoist Drum Stores and pays out the hoist rope.
Wire Rope Transfers the hoisting force between the drum and hook system.
Hook Block Connects the crane load line to the lifting accessories.
Counterweight Provides counterbalancing mass according to the crane design.
Outriggers Provide support and stability for cranes designed to use them.
Slewing System Rotates the crane upper structure.
Trolley Moves the load along a jib or bridge where fitted.
Load Moment / Rated Capacity System Monitors crane operating parameters and provides the functions specified by the manufacturer.

13. Crane Classification

Crane classification is not simply the maximum lifting capacity. Classification can consider crane type, duty, load spectrum, operating conditions and number of working cycles.

ISO 4301 Classification Family

Standard Subject
SASO GSO ISO 4301-1 General crane classification
SASO GSO ISO 4301-2 Mobile cranes
SASO GSO ISO 4301-3 Tower cranes
SASO GSO ISO 4301-4 Jib cranes
SASO GSO ISO 4301-5 Overhead travelling and portal bridge cranes
Do not use a crane classification group as a substitute for the manufacturer's rated capacity or load chart.

14. Crane Load Chart

The load chart is one of the most important documents for a crane lift. It defines rated capacities for specified configurations.

Load Chart Factors

  • Operating radius
  • Boom length
  • Boom angle
  • Counterweight
  • Outrigger configuration
  • Working surface / carrier configuration
  • Jib configuration
  • Parts of line
  • Hook block or hook weight
  • Wind limitations
Never calculate crane capacity from boom length and radius alone. The manufacturer's load chart for the exact crane configuration must be used.

15. Crane Inspection

Inspection requirements depend on crane type, manufacturer, applicable standard, Saudi requirements and site procedures.

General Pre-Use Checks

  • Crane identification
  • Rated capacity information
  • Current inspection status
  • Load chart availability
  • Wire rope condition
  • Hook condition
  • Hook latch where fitted
  • Boom condition
  • Hydraulic system
  • Brakes
  • Safety devices
  • Outriggers and supporting components
  • Counterweights
  • Electrical system
  • Alarms and indicators
  • Ground/support conditions

Examples of Defects Requiring Attention

  • Cracks
  • Structural deformation
  • Damaged wire rope
  • Damaged hook
  • Hydraulic leakage affecting safe operation
  • Damaged safety devices
  • Abnormal brake condition
  • Damaged outrigger components
  • Missing or unreadable identification
  • Unauthorized modification
A crane with a defect that can affect safe operation must not be used until it has been evaluated and released in accordance with the applicable inspection and site procedure.

Important Safety Notice

This page is a study reference and does not replace the crane manufacturer's manual, current load chart, inspection requirements, engineering assessment, approved lift plan or competent-person decision.

Crane capacity depends on the actual crane, configuration, radius, boom/jib arrangement, counterweight, support conditions and other manufacturer-specified limitations.

For Saudi Aramco work, follow the current applicable Saudi Aramco General Instructions, standards, procedures and technical alerts, together with applicable Saudi regulations and project requirements.
