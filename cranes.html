Cranes Study

Study notes for crane operations and lifting planning

← Back to Home

1. Mobile Cranes

Mobile cranes are used for many lifting operations. Study crane configuration, outriggers, counterweight, boom length, operating radius and rated capacity.

2. Crawler Cranes

Crawler cranes use tracked undercarriages and can be configured for different lifting applications. Study their load charts, configuration and site requirements.

3. Tower Cranes

Tower cranes are commonly used on construction projects. Study load charts, working radius, jib configuration, lifting zones and operating limitations.

4. Crane Load Charts

A crane load chart provides capacity information for specific crane configurations. Important factors can include radius, boom length, counterweight and configuration.

5. Operating Radius

Operating radius is an important factor in crane capacity. As radius changes, the allowable lifting capacity may also change. Always use the manufacturer's current load chart.

6. Outriggers and Ground Conditions

Crane setup must consider outrigger configuration, ground bearing capacity, support mats and site conditions. These requirements must be verified by competent personnel.

7. Crane Inspection

Cranes should be inspected and maintained according to applicable regulations, manufacturer requirements and site procedures. Defects must be properly assessed before operation.

8. Communication During Lifting

Clear communication between the crane operator, banksman, rigger and lifting team is essential. Agreed signals and communication methods should be established before the lift.

Important Safety Notice

This page is for study and reference only. Actual crane operations must follow the manufacturer's instructions, approved lift plan, applicable regulations, site procedures and competent-person requirements.
