Lifting Safety Reference

Lifting and rigging operations can expose personnel, equipment and facilities to serious hazards. Safe lifting requires proper planning, competent personnel, suitable equipment, effective communication and appropriate risk controls.

This page is intended for education and study. It does not replace a task-specific risk assessment, approved lift plan, engineering assessment, manufacturer instructions, applicable legislation, standards, site procedures or competent-person verification.

1. Basic Lifting Safety Principles

Plan Before You Lift

Every lifting operation should be properly planned before equipment is positioned and the load is lifted.

  • Understand the lifting task.
  • Identify the load.
  • Establish the load weight.
  • Determine the center of gravity.
  • Select suitable lifting equipment.
  • Assess the work area.
  • Identify hazards.
  • Establish control measures.
  • Confirm communication arrangements.
  • Define responsibilities.

Competent Personnel

Lifting operations should be planned, supervised and performed by personnel who are appropriately competent for their assigned roles.

  • Lifting supervisor
  • Crane operator
  • Rigger
  • Banksman or signal person
  • Lift planner
  • Engineer where required

2. Suspended Load Hazards

HIGH RISK

Suspended Loads

A suspended load can fall, swing, rotate or move unexpectedly. Personnel should not position themselves beneath suspended loads or within areas where an uncontrolled load could cause injury.

Controls

  • Establish an appropriate exclusion zone.
  • Keep unauthorized personnel away.
  • Use suitable lifting equipment.
  • Control load movement.
  • Use agreed communication methods.
  • Avoid unnecessary exposure to the suspended load.

Dropped Objects

A dropped load, loose component or failed lifting accessory can cause fatal injury and significant property damage.

Possible Causes

  • Incorrect rigging.
  • Equipment failure.
  • Overloading.
  • Improper connection.
  • Unsecured components.
  • Unexpected load movement.

Load Swing

Loads may swing because of wind, crane movement, acceleration, deceleration, incorrect rigging or an offset center of gravity.

Controls

  • Plan the lifting path.
  • Use appropriate load-control methods.
  • Maintain suitable clearances.
  • Control crane movements smoothly.
  • Consider environmental conditions.

3. Rigging Safety

Incorrect Rigging Configuration

Incorrect sling configuration can change the forces acting on lifting components and the load.

  • Verify the planned configuration.
  • Consider sling angles.
  • Confirm compatible connections.
  • Consider the load geometry.
  • Use manufacturer information.

Damaged Slings

Slings may be damaged by cuts, abrasion, broken wires, deformation, heat, chemicals, incorrect storage or unsuitable use.

Damaged or questionable lifting equipment should be controlled according to applicable procedures and should not be used unless properly assessed and accepted.

Sharp Edges

Sharp edges can damage synthetic slings and wire rope.

  • Identify sharp edges before lifting.
  • Use suitable edge protection where required.
  • Ensure protection remains correctly positioned.

Incorrect Sling Angles

The angle of sling legs can significantly affect the tension in each leg. Sling geometry must therefore be considered during lift planning and calculations.

4. Shackles, Hooks & Connections

Incorrect Connections

A connection that is incompatible, incorrectly assembled or improperly loaded can fail.

  • Use compatible components.
  • Verify identification and capacity information.
  • Check pins and connection points.
  • Follow manufacturer requirements.
  • Do not improvise connections.

Hook Hazards

Hooks can become hazardous when incorrectly loaded, overloaded, damaged or used with incompatible connections.

  • Verify the hook is suitable for the application.
  • Check identification.
  • Check for visible damage.
  • Check the latch where fitted.
  • Avoid improper loading of the hook.

5. Crane Safety

CRANE HAZARD

Crane Overturning

Crane stability can be affected by load radius, crane configuration, ground conditions, wind, incorrect setup and other factors.

  • Use the applicable crane load chart.
  • Confirm the crane configuration.
  • Verify operating radius.
  • Assess ground conditions.
  • Follow manufacturer requirements.

Ground Failure

Unsuitable ground can settle or fail under crane loads and supporting equipment.

  • Assess ground conditions.
  • Consider underground services.
  • Consider excavations and voids.
  • Use suitable support arrangements.
  • Follow engineering requirements where applicable.

Outrigger Hazards

Incorrect outrigger setup can affect crane stability.

  • Deploy according to the approved configuration.
  • Use suitable support arrangements.
  • Confirm adequate bearing conditions.
  • Follow the crane manufacturer's requirements.

Crane Collision

Cranes and loads may collide with structures, equipment, other cranes or personnel.

  • Plan the movement path.
  • Identify obstructions.
  • Maintain appropriate clearances.
  • Use a designated signal person where required.

6. Electrical Hazards

Overhead Power Lines

Contact between cranes, loads or lifting equipment and energized electrical lines can result in fatal electric shock, arc flash or equipment damage.

  • Identify overhead electrical hazards before lifting.
  • Maintain the required clearance.
  • Use site-approved controls.
  • Follow applicable electrical safety requirements.
  • Do not rely solely on visual estimation of distance.

7. Load Hazards

Overloading

Exceeding the applicable rated capacity of a crane or lifting component can result in equipment failure or loss of control.

  • Establish the load weight.
  • Verify applicable capacity information.
  • Consider configuration.
  • Consider lifting radius where applicable.
  • Do not exceed applicable limits.

Unknown Load Weight

The load weight should be reliably established before planning a lift. An unknown load weight can lead to incorrect equipment selection.

Unbalanced Loads

A load with an offset or unknown center of gravity may tilt or shift when lifted.

  • Identify the center of gravity.
  • Determine suitable lifting points.
  • Consider load geometry.
  • Plan the lifting arrangement.

Unexpected Load Movement

Loads can move because of snagging, wind, incorrect rigging, inadequate control or unexpected changes in conditions.

8. Personnel Hazards

Crush and Pinch Points

Personnel can be injured between loads, structures, cranes and lifting accessories.

  • Identify pinch points.
  • Keep hands and feet away from potential trapping points.
  • Maintain appropriate distance.
  • Use suitable load-control methods.

Caught-Between Hazards

Personnel should not position themselves between a moving load and a fixed object where they could become trapped.

Unauthorized Personnel

Only personnel required for the operation should enter controlled lifting areas.

9. Working at Height

Falls from Height

Some lifting activities require personnel to work at height or access elevated locations.

  • Identify fall hazards during planning.
  • Use appropriate fall-prevention systems.
  • Use approved access equipment.
  • Follow site work-at-height procedures.

Dropped Tools and Objects

Tools, shackles, pins and other objects can fall from elevated locations.

  • Control loose objects.
  • Establish exclusion zones below elevated work.
  • Use suitable tool-control methods where required.

10. Environmental Hazards

Wind

Wind can affect crane stability and cause suspended loads to move unpredictably.

  • Monitor applicable wind limits.
  • Follow crane manufacturer requirements.
  • Consider the load's surface area.
  • Stop operations when conditions exceed approved limits.

Rain and Poor Visibility

Poor visibility can affect communication, load control and awareness of surrounding hazards.

Lightning and Severe Weather

Severe weather can create significant risks to lifting operations. Operations should be suspended when required by site procedures, manufacturer requirements or the approved lifting plan.

Extreme Temperatures

Extreme temperatures can affect personnel, equipment and some materials.

11. Site Hazards

Excavations

Excavations and underground voids can affect ground stability.

  • Identify excavations before crane setup.
  • Maintain required distances.
  • Follow site engineering requirements.

Traffic

Vehicles and mobile equipment can create collision hazards around lifting areas.

  • Control vehicle movement.
  • Establish exclusion zones.
  • Use traffic management arrangements.

Structures and Obstructions

Buildings, pipe racks, scaffolding, equipment and other structures can restrict load and crane movement.

Poor Lighting

Insufficient lighting can reduce visibility of the load, rigging, signals and hazards.

12. Communication Safety

Hand Signals

Where hand signals are used, the lifting team should use an agreed and recognized signaling system.

Radio Communication

Where radios are required, communication should be established, tested and understood before the lifting operation.

  • Use agreed terminology.
  • Keep instructions clear.
  • Confirm critical instructions where necessary.
  • Maintain communication throughout the lift.

Designated Signal Person

The lifting team should establish who has authority to direct crane movements and how stop-work signals will be communicated.

13. Lift Planning Safety

Lift Plan

A lift plan should address the specific lifting operation and identify the equipment, load, lifting arrangement, hazards, controls and responsibilities.

Risk Assessment

The lifting team should identify foreseeable hazards and establish appropriate controls before the work begins.

Method Statement

The method statement should describe the planned work sequence, responsibilities and required controls where applicable.

Pre-Lift Meeting

The lifting team should understand the planned operation, communication arrangements, responsibilities and stop-work requirements before beginning the lift.

14. Inspection & Equipment Control

Pre-Use Inspection

  • Equipment identification
  • Rated capacity information
  • Hooks and latches
  • Shackles and pins
  • Slings
  • Wire ropes
  • Lifting points
  • Crane components
  • Safety devices where applicable

Damaged Equipment

Equipment that is damaged, unidentified or suspected to be unsuitable should be removed from service or otherwise controlled according to applicable procedures until properly assessed.

Inspection Records

Inspection and examination records should be maintained where required by applicable regulations, standards, manufacturer requirements and site procedures.

15. Personal Protective Equipment

Typical PPE

  • Safety helmet
  • Safety footwear
  • Suitable gloves
  • Eye protection
  • High-visibility clothing
  • Hearing protection where required
  • Fall protection where required
  • Task-specific PPE identified by the risk assessment

16. Housekeeping & Work Area Control

Clean Work Area

The lifting area should be kept organized and free of unnecessary obstructions that could interfere with personnel, equipment or load movement.

Trip Hazards

Cables, ropes, tools, materials and other objects can create trip hazards around lifting areas.

17. Simultaneous Operations

SIMOPS

Simultaneous operations can create additional hazards when lifting is performed near other work activities.

  • Identify nearby activities.
  • Coordinate work groups.
  • Control conflicting activities.
  • Maintain appropriate exclusion zones.
  • Coordinate permits and communications where applicable.

18. Emergency Response

Emergency Planning

The lifting team should understand the emergency arrangements before starting the operation.

  • Emergency communication method
  • Emergency contact arrangements
  • First-aid arrangements
  • Access for emergency services
  • Emergency assembly arrangements
  • Response to dropped loads
  • Response to equipment failure
  • Response to severe weather
  • Response to electrical incidents

Stop Work

Any member of the lifting team should have a clear method to stop the operation when an unsafe condition develops or when the actual conditions no longer match the approved plan.

19. Pre-Lift Safety Checklist

Before the Lift

  • Lift plan reviewed.
  • Risk assessment completed.
  • Method statement reviewed where applicable.
  • Load weight established.
  • Center of gravity considered.
  • Suitable crane selected.
  • Applicable load chart verified.
  • Operating radius confirmed.
  • Crane configuration confirmed.
  • Suitable lifting accessories selected.
  • Lifting accessories identified.
  • Equipment condition checked.
  • Ground conditions assessed.
  • Outrigger/support arrangements verified.
  • Overhead hazards identified.
  • Electrical hazards identified.
  • Load path established.
  • Exclusion zone established.
  • Communication method agreed.
  • Weather conditions checked.
  • Personnel briefed.
  • Emergency arrangements understood.

20. During the Lift

During Operation

  • Maintain communication.
  • Follow the approved lifting sequence.
  • Monitor the load.
  • Monitor crane condition and movement.
  • Maintain exclusion zones.
  • Control load movement.
  • Avoid unnecessary personnel exposure.
  • Monitor environmental conditions.
  • Stop if conditions change significantly.
  • Stop if the load behaves unexpectedly.
  • Stop if communication is lost when communication is essential.
  • Stop if equipment shows signs of abnormal behavior.

21. Post-Lift Safety

After the Load Is Placed

  • Confirm the load is stable.
  • Ensure the load is adequately supported.
  • Release lifting equipment in a controlled manner.
  • Remove accessories safely.
  • Inspect equipment for damage.
  • Store equipment appropriately.
  • Record issues where required.

22. Safety Golden Rules

Golden Rules for Lifting Operations

  • Never lift without proper planning.
  • Never exceed applicable equipment limits.
  • Never stand beneath a suspended load.
  • Never use unidentified or unsuitable lifting equipment.
  • Never ignore damaged equipment.
  • Never enter an uncontrolled lifting area.
  • Never ignore changing weather conditions.
  • Never bypass safety procedures.
  • Always maintain effective communication.
  • Always follow the approved lifting plan.
  • Always stop work when an unsafe condition develops.

Important Safety Notice

This website is intended for education and study purposes only. It does not replace an approved lift plan, task-specific risk assessment, engineering assessment, manufacturer instructions, applicable laws, regulations, standards, site procedures or competent person verification.

Actual lifting operations must be planned, supervised and controlled by appropriately competent personnel using equipment suitable for the specific task and conditions.
