Lift Planning Study

Study notes for planning safe lifting operations

1. What is a Lift Plan?

A lift plan describes how a lifting operation is intended to be carried out. It should contain the information, controls and responsibilities required for the particular lift.

2. Load Information

Before planning a lift, establish reliable information about:

  • Verified load weight
  • Dimensions
  • Center of gravity
  • Lifting points
  • Load condition
  • Any special handling requirements

3. Rigging Arrangement

The rigging arrangement should consider suitable lifting equipment, equipment capacity, connection points, sling configuration, angles and load distribution.

4. Crane Selection

Crane selection should consider the required capacity, operating radius, boom configuration, site access, ground conditions and the manufacturer's load chart.

5. Risk Assessment

Identify hazards associated with the lifting operation and establish appropriate control measures. Consider suspended loads, people, structures, power lines, weather, ground conditions and nearby activities.

6. Method Statement

The method statement should explain the planned sequence of work, responsibilities, equipment, communication, controls and emergency arrangements.

7. Exclusion Zone

Establish an appropriate controlled area around the lifting operation to prevent unauthorized people from entering hazardous areas.

8. Communication

Before starting, the lifting team should agree on the communication method, signals and responsibilities between the operator, banksman, rigger and other involved personnel.

9. Weather and Site Conditions

Weather, wind, visibility, ground conditions and site restrictions should be considered before and during the lift. Manufacturer limits and site requirements must be followed.

10. Pre-Lift Meeting

A pre-lift meeting can be used to review the plan, responsibilities, hazards, communication methods, equipment and emergency arrangements with the lifting team.

Important Safety Notice

This page is for study and reference only. An actual lifting operation must be supported by an approved lift plan, appropriate engineering calculations, manufacturer information, applicable regulations and competent-person verification.
