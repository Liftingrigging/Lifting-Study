About GI 7.029

This study page covers important rigging hardware concepts, including lifting slings, shackles, hooks, master links, identification, rated capacity, inspection and safe use.

Important: This page is a study reference only. Always use the current approved Saudi Aramco requirements, applicable standards, manufacturer's instructions, certificates and competent-person verification for actual lifting operations.

1. What Is Rigging Hardware?

Rigging hardware consists of equipment used to connect, support, attach, control or transfer loads during lifting operations.

Examples include:

  • Wire rope slings
  • Webbing slings
  • Round slings
  • Chain slings
  • Shackles
  • Hooks
  • Master links
  • Connecting links
  • Swivels
  • Plate clamps
  • Lifting beams
  • Other approved lifting devices

2. Working Load Limit

Working Load Limit (WLL) is the rated maximum load that an item is designed to support under specified conditions.

The rating must be considered together with the equipment configuration, connection method, angle, environment and manufacturer's instructions.

The complete lifting arrangement must not be treated as having a capacity greater than its applicable limiting component.

3. Safe Working Load

Safe Working Load (SWL) is commonly used to describe the maximum permitted load under defined conditions.

Lifting System Capacity = Limited by the Applicable Weakest Component

In study material, WLL, SWL and rated working capacity may be encountered depending on the equipment and governing document. Always use the terminology and rating specified by the applicable manufacturer or standard.

4. Wire Rope Slings

Wire rope slings are commonly used for industrial lifting applications.

Inspection Points

  • Broken or damaged wires
  • Severe abrasion
  • Crushing
  • Kinking
  • Birdcaging
  • Corrosion
  • Heat damage
  • Damaged end fittings
  • Deformation
  • Missing identification
A wire rope sling with questionable condition or unidentified rating should not be used until its status has been determined according to the applicable procedure.

5. Synthetic Webbing Slings

Webbing slings are lightweight and flexible lifting accessories.

Typical Damage to Look For

  • Cuts
  • Tears
  • Abrasion
  • Burns
  • Heat damage
  • Chemical damage
  • Damaged stitching
  • Deformation
  • Missing or unreadable identification
Synthetic slings must be protected from sharp edges and other hazards capable of damaging the sling.

6. Round Slings

Round slings contain load-bearing fibers protected by an outer cover.

  • Inspect the outer cover.
  • Look for cuts and abrasion.
  • Check for exposed fibers.
  • Check for heat or chemical damage.
  • Verify identification and rated capacity.
  • Use appropriate protection around sharp edges.

7. Chain Slings

Chain slings are commonly used where durable lifting arrangements are required.

Inspection Areas

  • Chain links
  • Hooks
  • Master links
  • Connecting links
  • Identification
  • Wear
  • Stretching or deformation
  • Cracks
  • Corrosion

8. Sling Identification

Lifting slings should have permanent identification appropriate to the applicable requirements.

Identification may include information such as:

  • Manufacturer
  • Rated capacity
  • Configuration
  • Size
  • Material
  • Applicable identification number
If required identification is missing, damaged or unreadable, follow the applicable equipment-control procedure before using the sling.

9. Shackles

Shackles are connecting devices commonly used between lifting components.

Types

  • Anchor / bow shackles
  • Chain / dee shackles
  • Screw-pin shackles
  • Safety-type shackles

Inspection

  • Check body condition.
  • Check pin condition.
  • Check for cracks.
  • Check for deformation.
  • Check for excessive wear.
  • Verify identification and rating.
  • Verify correct assembly.

10. Shackle Loading

Shackles must be loaded according to their approved configuration.

  • Do not exceed the applicable rated capacity.
  • Avoid side loading unless specifically permitted.
  • Ensure the pin is correctly installed.
  • Ensure connected components are compatible.
  • Follow manufacturer instructions.

11. Hooks

Hooks connect the crane or lifting device to the rigging arrangement.

Inspection Points

  • Hook body
  • Throat opening
  • Twisting
  • Cracks
  • Wear
  • Corrosion
  • Safety latch where fitted
  • Identification
The load should be properly seated in the hook. Loading the tip of the hook is not an acceptable substitute for proper hook engagement.

12. Master Links

Master links connect sling legs or assemblies to the crane hook or other approved connection.

  • Verify rated capacity.
  • Inspect for deformation.
  • Inspect for cracks and excessive wear.
  • Verify compatible connections.
  • Check identification.

13. Connecting Links

Connecting links join compatible components in a lifting assembly.

  • Use only compatible components.
  • Verify rated capacity.
  • Inspect for wear and deformation.
  • Check correct assembly.
  • Follow manufacturer's requirements.

14. D/d Ratio

The D/d ratio is an important concept when a sling body bends around a support.

D/d = D ÷ d

Where:

  • D = diameter of curvature around which the sling body is bent
  • d = diameter of the sling body
The applicable sling capacity for a particular bend and configuration must be determined from the relevant rating information and requirements.

15. Sling Angle

Sling angle affects the tension carried by each sling leg.

T = W ÷ (2 × sin θ)

This is a simplified symmetrical two-leg study relationship.

  • T = tension in each leg
  • W = load weight
  • θ = sling angle from the horizontal
Do not use a classroom formula as the sole basis for selecting sling capacity. Verify the manufacturer's rated capacity for the actual configuration.

16. Choker Hitch

A choker hitch wraps the sling around the load and connects the sling back to itself or an approved connection point.

The rated capacity depends on the sling type, hitch, configuration and applicable manufacturer's information.

Do not assume that the vertical-rated capacity of a sling is automatically its choker-rated capacity.

17. Basket Hitch

In a basket arrangement, the sling passes underneath the load and both ends connect to the lifting point.

Capacity depends on the specific sling, configuration, included angle and manufacturer's rating.

18. Sharp Edge Protection

Sharp edges can cut or damage lifting slings.

  • Identify edges before lifting.
  • Use suitable protection.
  • Prevent sling movement over sharp edges.
  • Consider crushing between the load and sling.
  • Use manufacturer-approved methods.

19. Prohibited Sling Practices

Lifting slings must not be modified or repaired using unauthorized methods.

Study and remember that improper practices such as knotting, unauthorized re-splicing or unauthorized use of wire-rope clips can compromise sling integrity.

Follow the applicable Saudi Aramco requirements and manufacturer's approved methods for shortening, adjusting, repairing or replacing lifting equipment.

20. Lifting Equipment Certificates

Required inspection, testing and certification documentation should be available according to the applicable requirements.

  • Equipment identification
  • Rated capacity
  • Inspection status
  • Test documentation where required
  • Certificate traceability
  • Manufacturer information

21. Lifting Hardware Selection

Selection should consider the complete lifting arrangement.

Factor Study Question
Load What is the verified load weight?
Geometry Where is the center of gravity?
Connection Are all components compatible?
Capacity Is the rated capacity suitable?
Angle How does the configuration affect loading?
Edges Is protection required?
Environment Are temperature, chemicals or other conditions relevant?
Inspection Is the equipment in acceptable condition?

22. Inspection Before Use

Before using lifting hardware, verify its condition and identification according to the applicable inspection process.

  • Correct equipment
  • Correct capacity
  • Correct configuration
  • Correct identification
  • Acceptable physical condition
  • Required inspection status
  • Required certification

23. Weakest Link Principle

A lifting assembly is only as strong as the limiting component under the actual loading conditions.

System Capacity ≤ Applicable Capacity of the Limiting Component

This concept should be applied to the complete load path, including the crane hook, connecting hardware, slings and lifting points.

24. Common Rigging Hazards

  • Overloading
  • Incorrect sling angle
  • Damaged slings
  • Damaged shackles
  • Incorrect hook engagement
  • Sharp edges
  • Unbalanced loads
  • Unexpected load movement
  • Pinch points
  • Suspended-load exposure
  • Incorrect communication
  • Unapproved equipment modification
