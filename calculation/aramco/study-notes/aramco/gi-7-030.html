Inspection & Testing Requirements for Elevating/Lifting Equipment

About GI 7.030

This study page explains the major concepts involved in inspection, testing, certification and control of lifting and elevating equipment.

Important: This is a study reference and not a replacement for the current Saudi Aramco General Instruction, applicable legal requirements, equipment manufacturer's instructions, inspection procedures or competent-person determination.

1. Purpose of Inspection

Inspection helps identify equipment conditions that could affect safe lifting operation.

  • Identify damage.
  • Identify excessive wear.
  • Identify deformation.
  • Verify identification.
  • Verify inspection status.
  • Confirm equipment remains suitable for its intended use.
  • Identify conditions requiring removal from service.

2. Types of Inspection

Pre-Use Inspection

An inspection performed before equipment is used to identify visible defects or unsafe conditions.

Periodic Inspection

A scheduled inspection performed at the required interval according to applicable requirements.

Special Inspection

Additional inspection may be required after an incident, overload, major repair, modification or other event affecting equipment integrity.

3. Crane Inspection

Crane inspections should address the equipment components and safety systems applicable to the specific crane.

  • Structural members
  • Boom
  • Jib
  • Hook
  • Wire rope
  • Drums
  • Sheaves
  • Brakes
  • Controls
  • Outriggers
  • Safety devices
  • Load moment/rated capacity systems where fitted
  • Electrical and hydraulic systems as applicable
  • Required documentation

4. Wire Rope Inspection

Wire rope should be examined for conditions that could compromise its integrity.

  • Broken wires
  • Corrosion
  • Wear
  • Crushing
  • Kinking
  • Birdcaging
  • Heat damage
  • Diameter reduction
  • Distortion
  • Damaged end connections
Do not determine whether a wire rope is acceptable using a single visual observation alone. Applicable inspection criteria and competent-person evaluation must be followed.

5. Hook Inspection

  • Check for cracks.
  • Check for deformation.
  • Check throat opening.
  • Check for excessive wear.
  • Check for twisting.
  • Check the latch where fitted.
  • Verify identification and rated capacity.

6. Sheave Inspection

Sheaves guide wire rope and are an important part of crane reeving systems.

  • Check groove condition.
  • Check for excessive wear.
  • Check alignment.
  • Check bearings where applicable.
  • Check for cracks or damage.
  • Check compatibility with the wire rope.

7. Drum Inspection

  • Inspect drum condition.
  • Check rope spooling.
  • Check for damage.
  • Check rope attachment.
  • Check applicable minimum rope requirements.
  • Follow manufacturer instructions.

8. Lifting Slings

Wire Rope Slings

  • Broken wires
  • Corrosion
  • Crushing
  • Kinking
  • Birdcaging
  • Heat damage
  • Damaged fittings
  • Missing identification

Webbing and Round Slings

  • Cuts
  • Abrasion
  • Burn damage
  • Chemical damage
  • Damaged stitching
  • Exposed fibers
  • Missing identification

Chain Slings

  • Cracks
  • Excessive wear
  • Stretching
  • Deformation
  • Corrosion
  • Damaged hooks
  • Damaged connecting links

9. Shackle Inspection

  • Check the body.
  • Check the pin.
  • Check threads where applicable.
  • Check for deformation.
  • Check for cracks.
  • Check for excessive wear.
  • Verify identification.
  • Verify rated capacity.

10. Master Link Inspection

  • Check for deformation.
  • Check for cracks.
  • Check for excessive wear.
  • Check for corrosion.
  • Verify identification.
  • Verify rated capacity.

11. Lifting Beam and Spreader Beam Inspection

  • Inspect structural members.
  • Check lifting points.
  • Check weld areas.
  • Check pins and connections.
  • Check for deformation.
  • Verify identification.
  • Verify rated capacity.
  • Confirm required certification.
Do not use fabricated lifting beams or frames unless they have been properly designed, approved, inspected and certified according to the applicable requirements.

12. Below-the-Hook Devices

Below-the-hook lifting devices can include:

  • Plate clamps
  • Magnets
  • Vacuum lifters
  • Drum lifters
  • Pipe lifters
  • Container lifting devices
  • Special lifting frames

Each device should be inspected according to its design, manufacturer requirements and applicable inspection criteria.

13. Identification

Lifting equipment should have the required identification and rating information.

  • Equipment identification
  • Manufacturer
  • Rated capacity
  • Size or model where applicable
  • Inspection information where required
  • Traceability information where required
Equipment that cannot be positively identified or whose rating cannot be established should be handled according to the applicable equipment-control procedure.

14. Testing

Testing requirements depend on the type of lifting equipment and the applicable requirements.

Testing may be associated with:

  • Initial certification
  • Periodic requirements
  • New equipment
  • Major repair
  • Modification
  • Re-certification
  • Specific inspection findings
Do not invent or apply a generic test load percentage to every type of lifting equipment. The required test method and load must come from the applicable standard, manufacturer and governing procedure.

15. Proof Load Testing

Proof-load testing is a controlled test used where required to verify equipment performance or capacity under specified conditions.

The applicable testing organization, procedure, test load, equipment configuration and acceptance criteria must be determined from the governing requirements.

16. Certificates

Required certificates and inspection records provide evidence that equipment has undergone the required inspection or test.

  • Equipment identification
  • Inspection date
  • Testing information where applicable
  • Rated capacity
  • Equipment description
  • Certification information
  • Traceability

17. Equipment Status

Lifting equipment should have a clearly understood status according to the applicable equipment-control system.

Status Study Meaning
Accepted Equipment has the required status for intended use.
Due for Inspection Inspection status requires attention according to the applicable interval.
Rejected Equipment is not to be used until properly dispositioned.
Quarantined Equipment is segregated pending evaluation or disposition.

18. Removal From Service

Equipment should be removed from service when it has defects or conditions that make it unsuitable for use.

  • Severe damage
  • Cracks
  • Excessive wear
  • Deformation
  • Missing required identification
  • Failed inspection
  • Failed test
  • Unknown certification status
  • Other conditions specified by the applicable procedure
Do not return rejected equipment to service simply because the defect appears minor. Follow the approved inspection and disposition process.

19. Crane Safety Devices

Where fitted, crane safety devices should be maintained and checked according to the manufacturer's requirements.

  • Load moment systems
  • Rated capacity indicators
  • Limit switches
  • Anti-two-block devices
  • Emergency stops
  • Alarms
  • Level indicators
  • Other manufacturer-specified safety systems

20. Inspection After an Incident

Equipment involved in an overload, shock loading, collision, dropped load or other significant event may require additional inspection before being returned to service.

  • Stop use when required.
  • Secure the equipment.
  • Report the event.
  • Conduct the required inspection.
  • Repair or replace equipment as required.
  • Re-certify where required.

21. Inspection Records

Inspection and testing records should be controlled and traceable according to the applicable system.

  • Equipment identification
  • Inspection date
  • Inspection result
  • Defects identified
  • Corrective action
  • Certificate information
  • Next required inspection where applicable

22. Common Inspection Errors

  • Using equipment with missing identification.
  • Ignoring visible damage.
  • Using expired or unavailable certification.
  • Failing to inspect connecting hardware.
  • Ignoring wire-rope deterioration.
  • Using damaged synthetic slings.
  • Failing to inspect hooks.
  • Failing to check lifting points.
  • Assuming equipment is acceptable because it worked previously.
  • Returning rejected equipment to service without proper disposition.

23. Inspector Study Points

  • Know the equipment being inspected.
  • Know the applicable inspection criteria.
  • Know the manufacturer's requirements.
  • Understand equipment identification.
  • Understand rated capacity.
  • Recognize common defects.
  • Understand documentation requirements.
  • Understand removal-from-service requirements.
  • Maintain inspection traceability.
