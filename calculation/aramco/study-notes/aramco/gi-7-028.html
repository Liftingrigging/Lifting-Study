About GI 7.028

This study page provides a structured overview of crane-lift planning and procedural topics associated with Saudi Aramco lifting operations.

Important Safety Notice: This page is a study aid and does not replace the current Saudi Aramco General Instruction, approved lift plan, manufacturer's instructions, applicable regulations, project requirements or competent-person review.

1. What Is a Crane Lift?

A crane lift is an operation in which a crane and associated lifting equipment are used to raise, move, position or lower a load.

A safe crane lift requires consideration of the complete lifting system, including:

  • Load
  • Crane
  • Rigging equipment
  • Ground conditions
  • Load radius
  • Crane configuration
  • Environmental conditions
  • Personnel
  • Communication
  • Lift path
  • Landing area

2. Crane Lift Planning

Lift planning should establish how the load will be safely picked, moved and placed.

Step 1: Identify the load and its characteristics.
Step 2: Establish the verified load weight.
Step 3: Determine the center of gravity where necessary.
Step 4: Select an appropriate crane.
Step 5: Determine the operating radius and configuration.
Step 6: Select suitable lifting accessories.
Step 7: Assess the ground and work area.
Step 8: Establish the lifting and travel path.
Step 9: Establish communication arrangements.
Step 10: Review hazards and required controls.

3. Load Information

Important load information may include:

  • Load description
  • Load weight
  • Dimensions
  • Center of gravity
  • Number and location of lifting points
  • Required lifting accessories
  • Potential for movement or rotation
  • Fragility or special handling requirements
Never rely on an unsupported estimate when verified load information is required for the lift.

4. Gross Load

The crane may be required to handle more than simply the weight of the object being lifted.

Gross Load = Load Weight + Applicable Lifting Equipment Weight

Depending on the lift and crane configuration, the applicable lifted equipment may include items such as spreader beams, lifting beams, hooks, below-the-hook devices and rigging accessories.

Use the load definition required by the applicable crane load chart and lift procedure. Do not assume that every crane manufacturer defines rated capacity in exactly the same way.

5. Crane Selection

Crane selection depends on the specific lift and crane configuration.

Factor Why It Matters
Load weight Determines required lifting capacity.
Operating radius Crane capacity commonly changes with radius.
Boom length Affects reach and available capacity.
Boom angle Changes crane geometry and load-chart capacity.
Counterweight Configuration can affect rated capacity.
Outriggers Configuration and setup affect crane capacity and stability.
Ground conditions Crane stability depends on suitable support conditions.
Wind Wind can affect crane and load behavior.

6. Load Radius

Radius is the horizontal distance relevant to the crane's lifting geometry, commonly considered from the crane's center of rotation to the load's center of gravity or other point defined by the applicable load chart.

Always use the exact radius definition and load-chart conditions specified by the crane manufacturer.

7. Crane Load Chart

The load chart provides capacity information for specific crane configurations and operating conditions.

Load-chart information can depend on:

  • Crane model
  • Boom length
  • Boom angle
  • Radius
  • Counterweight
  • Outrigger configuration
  • Jib configuration
  • Parts of line
  • Operating area
  • Manufacturer limitations
The crane manufacturer's current load chart for the actual crane configuration is the controlling source for crane capacity.

8. Crane Capacity Utilization

A useful study relationship is:

Utilization (%) = (Applied Load ÷ Available Rated Capacity) × 100

The exact load and capacity definitions must be taken from the applicable crane load chart and lift procedure.

9. Crane Configuration

Before a lift, verify the crane configuration against the applicable load chart.

  • Crane model
  • Counterweight configuration
  • Outrigger configuration
  • Boom length
  • Jib configuration
  • Hook block
  • Parts of line
  • Operating radius
  • Operating area

10. Outriggers and Crane Setup

Mobile cranes may require outriggers or other stabilizing systems according to the manufacturer's instructions.

  • Position the crane according to the approved lift plan.
  • Verify the required outrigger configuration.
  • Assess ground bearing conditions.
  • Use suitable support systems where required.
  • Keep the crane within its approved operating configuration.
Do not assume that a visually firm surface is adequate for crane loading. Ground suitability should be assessed by the responsible competent/engineering personnel as required.

11. Ground Bearing

A simplified study relationship for average ground pressure is:

Pressure = Force ÷ Area
P = F ÷ A

Rearranging:

Required Area = Force ÷ Allowable Pressure
A = F ÷ Pallow
Actual crane ground-bearing assessments require consideration of the crane manufacturer's data, support arrangement, soil/geotechnical information and applicable engineering requirements.

12. Lifting Accessories

The rigging arrangement should be suitable for the load and lifting configuration.

  • Wire rope slings
  • Webbing slings
  • Round slings
  • Chain slings
  • Shackles
  • Hooks
  • Master links
  • Lifting beams
  • Spreader beams
  • Plate clamps
  • Other approved below-the-hook devices

13. Rigging Inspection

Rigging should be inspected before use according to applicable requirements.

  • Verify identification.
  • Verify rated capacity.
  • Check for damage.
  • Check hooks and latches.
  • Check shackles and pins.
  • Check slings for deterioration.
  • Check lifting devices for deformation.
  • Remove questionable equipment from service according to procedure.

14. Crane Inspection

Crane inspection and certification requirements should be verified before the crane is used.

  • Required inspection status
  • Certification/documentation
  • Safety devices
  • Hooks
  • Wire rope
  • Brakes
  • Controls
  • Outriggers
  • Structural components
  • Load moment / rated capacity systems where fitted

15. Lift Path

The planned movement of the load should be reviewed before the lift begins.

  • Identify obstacles.
  • Identify overhead hazards.
  • Identify structures and equipment.
  • Identify personnel access areas.
  • Establish the landing location.
  • Control unauthorized access.
  • Consider load swing and rotation.

16. Critical Lifts

Critical lifts require enhanced planning and controls in accordance with applicable Saudi Aramco and project requirements.

Study the following information:

  • Load description
  • Load weight
  • Load dimensions
  • Center of gravity
  • Lifting devices
  • Crane identification
  • Crane configuration
  • Boom length
  • Jib configuration
  • Hook block information
  • Radius
  • Ground conditions
  • Weather conditions
  • Inspection status
  • Communication arrangements
  • Exclusion zone

17. Tandem / Multiple Crane Lifts

Multiple-crane lifting is a specialized operation requiring detailed engineering and planning.

Important study concepts include:

  • Load distribution
  • Center of gravity
  • Crane capacity
  • Crane geometry
  • Synchronization
  • Communication
  • Ground conditions
  • Load path
  • Contingency planning

Tandem crane lifts must not be performed from simple classroom formulas alone. They require an appropriate engineered plan and competent-person control.

18. Wind and Weather

Wind can significantly affect both crane operations and suspended loads, particularly loads with large surface areas.

  • Monitor weather conditions.
  • Follow the crane manufacturer's wind limitations.
  • Consider the load's exposed surface area.
  • Consider sudden changes in weather.
  • Stop the lift when required by the approved procedure.

19. Power Lines

Crane operations near electrical power lines require specific planning and controls.

  • Identify power lines before the lift.
  • Determine applicable approach restrictions.
  • Establish controls and exclusion zones.
  • Consider crane boom movement.
  • Consider load movement.
  • Follow applicable Saudi Aramco requirements.
Treat overhead electrical lines as a serious lifting hazard. The applicable site procedure and electrical safety requirements must be followed.

20. Banksman / Signaller

The signaller provides the crane operator with agreed signals for crane movements when required.

  • Maintain a suitable position.
  • Remain visible to the operator when using hand signals.
  • Use agreed signals.
  • Use radio communication where required.
  • Maintain awareness of the load and surrounding area.
  • Stop the operation when an unsafe condition develops.

21. Blind Lifts

A blind lift occurs when the crane operator cannot directly see the load or landing area.

Blind lifts require reliable communication and appropriate controls.

  • Use an agreed communication system.
  • Ensure competent signalling personnel are assigned.
  • Confirm the communication method before starting.
  • Stop if communication is lost.
  • Control personnel access to the lift area.

22. Pre-Lift Meeting

A pre-lift meeting should ensure that personnel understand the approved lifting method and their responsibilities.

  • Review the lift plan.
  • Review load information.
  • Review crane configuration.
  • Review rigging arrangement.
  • Review communication signals.
  • Review exclusion zones.
  • Review hazards.
  • Confirm responsibilities.
  • Confirm emergency arrangements.

23. Stop Work Conditions

The lifting operation should be stopped when an unsafe or uncontrolled condition develops.

  • Communication is lost.
  • Unexpected load movement occurs.
  • Rigging becomes damaged or unstable.
  • Weather exceeds applicable limits.
  • Personnel enter the controlled area.
  • Ground conditions become unsafe.
  • Crane alarms or abnormal conditions occur.
  • The lift no longer matches the approved plan.
