Calculation method

Static Crane A = W × (distance B to COG) ÷ total distance
Static Crane B = W × (distance A to COG) ÷ total distance

W is total load weight. These equations provide static reactions for a rigid load supported by two vertical forces. Angle alone does not change these reactions in that model.

Illustrative transfer = W × p × sin(2θ)
Crane A angle load = static A − transfer
Crane B angle load = static B + transfer

Here p is the user-entered transfer fraction and θ is the angle in degrees. This assumed trend increases Crane B's load from 0° to 45°, then increases Crane A's load from 45° to 90°. It is a user-configurable illustration, not a standard or a substitute for a geometry-based force analysis.

Safety: The transfer model must not be used to authorize a lift. Real angle-dependent load transfer requires the actual hook coordinates, sling directions, lifting-point geometry, crane movements, ground-contact conditions and relevant dynamic effects. A competent lifting engineer must determine the appropriate method and validate every stage against certified crane load charts and the approved lift plan.
