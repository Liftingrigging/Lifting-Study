This section is designed as a structured study reference for lifting and rigging subjects commonly encountered in professional training, inspection and lifting operations.

Topics include rigging equipment, crane operations, calculations, load control, inspection, planning and safety.

⚠️ Important Safety Notice

This material is for study and educational purposes. It is not a substitute for an approved lift plan, engineering calculation, manufacturer's instructions, load charts, applicable standards, site procedures or competent-person verification.

1. Load Calculations

Weight

Basic relationship between mass and weight.

W = m × g
  • W = weight
  • m = mass
  • g ≈ 9.81 m/s²

Load Moment

Moment created by a load at a horizontal distance.

M = W × R
  • M = moment
  • W = load
  • R = radius

Center of Gravity

For simplified discrete loads:

x = Σ(W × x) ÷ ΣW

The actual centre of gravity should be verified where uncertainty exists.

2. Sling Calculations

Two-Leg Sling

Simplified symmetrical two-leg arrangement. Angle measured from horizontal.

T = W ÷ (2 × sin θ)

Sling Angle

As sling angle decreases from horizontal, leg tension increases.

Angle Factor = 1 ÷ (2 × sin θ)

Vertical Angle

If the angle is measured from vertical:

T = W ÷ (2 × cos θ)

4. D/d Ratio

D/d Ratio

D/d = D ÷ d

D represents the diameter of curvature around which the sling or wire rope is bent, while d represents the relevant sling or wire-rope diameter.

Capacity effects must be determined from the applicable manufacturer data or governing standard. Do not assume a capacity factor without the applicable source.

7. Crane Calculations

Crane Capacity

Always use the applicable manufacturer's load chart.

  • Crane configuration
  • Boom length
  • Boom angle
  • Radius
  • Counterweight
  • Outriggers

Crane Utilization

Utilization = Actual Load ÷ Applicable Capacity × 100

The applicable capacity must come from the correct configuration and load chart.

Gross Load

Gross Load = Payload + Rigging + Lifting Device + Other Suspended Items

8. Ground Bearing

Ground Pressure

P = F ÷ A
  • P = pressure
  • F = force
  • A = bearing area

Required Area

A = F ÷ Pallow

The allowable bearing pressure must be established from appropriate site engineering information.

9. Reeving & Mechanical Advantage

Ideal Mechanical Advantage

MA ≈ Number of Supporting Rope Parts

This is an idealized relationship. Real systems are affected by sheave and rope efficiency, reeving arrangement and equipment limitations.

10. Tandem Lifting
