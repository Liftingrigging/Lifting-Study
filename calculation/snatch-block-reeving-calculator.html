Basic Study Formulas

Ideal Mechanical Advantage = Number of Supporting Rope Parts
Ideal Rope Tension = Load ÷ Number of Supporting Parts
Estimated Actual Rope Tension = Ideal Rope Tension ÷ Efficiency

The number of supporting rope parts is sometimes referred to as the number of parts of line.

Example Calculation

Load = 10 t Supporting Rope Parts = 4 Efficiency = 100%

Mechanical Advantage = 4

Ideal Rope Tension: 10 ÷ 4 = 2.50 t

Efficiency Example

Load = 10 t Supporting Parts = 4 Efficiency = 90%

Ideal Tension: 10 ÷ 4 = 2.50 t

Estimated Tension: 2.50 ÷ 0.90 = 2.78 t
The efficiency input is a simplified study allowance. Actual reeving efficiency depends on sheave and bearing condition, rope construction, sheave geometry, friction, alignment and the specific equipment.

Important Reeving Concepts

  • More supporting rope parts can increase ideal mechanical advantage.
  • Rope tension is not automatically equal to the lifted load.
  • The actual rope path must be identified correctly.
  • The dead end and running end arrangements can affect the force distribution.
  • Sheave friction reduces the ideal mechanical advantage.
  • Rope fleet angle and sheave condition can affect rope performance.
  • The snatch block itself has a rated capacity that must be verified.
Important Safety Notice

This calculator is a simplified study tool only.

It does not determine the permissible load of a crane, wire rope, snatch block, sheave, hook, anchor point or other lifting equipment.

Actual reeving arrangements must be checked against the specific crane and manufacturer's documentation, including the approved reeving configuration and rated capacities.

Do not use the calculated mechanical advantage by itself to approve a lifting operation.

Actual lifting operations require an approved lift plan, manufacturer information and competent-person verification.
