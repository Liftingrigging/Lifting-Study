Calculation Questions and Answers

These worked examples are for study and revision. Actual lifting operations must follow the approved lift plan, equipment load charts, manufacturer instructions and applicable site requirements.

1. Calculate the weight of a steel plate

Question: A steel plate is 2 m long, 1 m wide and 20 mm thick. Calculate its approximate weight. Use steel density = 7,850 kg/m³.

Answer:

Convert thickness: 20 mm = 0.02 m

Volume = Length × Width × Thickness

Volume = 2 × 1 × 0.02 = 0.04 m³

Mass = 0.04 × 7,850 = 314 kg

Result: approximately 314 kg, excluding attachments or other added weight.

2. Calculate the weight of a rectangular concrete block

Question: A concrete block is 3 m long, 2 m wide and 1 m high. Use a concrete density of 2,400 kg/m³.

Answer:

Volume = 3 × 2 × 1 = 6 m³

Mass = 6 × 2,400 = 14,400 kg

Result: 14,400 kg or 14.4 tonnes.

3. Calculate the force in a two-leg sling

Question: A 2,000 kg load is shared equally by two sling legs. Each leg makes an angle of 60° from the horizontal. Calculate the ideal tension in each leg.

Answer:

Approximate load force = mass × g

W = 2,000 × 9.81 = 19,620 N = 19.62 kN

T = W ÷ (2 × sin θ)

T = 19.62 ÷ (2 × sin 60°)

T = 19.62 ÷ 1.732 = 11.33 kN

Result: approximately 11.33 kN per leg under the stated ideal assumptions.

4. Calculate sling tension at a lower angle

Question: A 1,000 kg load is equally shared by two sling legs, each at 30° above the horizontal. Calculate ideal tension in each leg.

Answer:

W = 1,000 × 9.81 = 9.81 kN

T = W ÷ (2 × sin θ)

T = 9.81 ÷ (2 × sin 30°) = 9.81 kN

Result: 9.81 kN per leg.

A smaller sling angle increases leg tension. Never select a sling from this calculation alone; confirm rated capacity, hitch, angle limits and load distribution.

5. Convert kilograms to tonnes

Question: Convert 7,500 kg into metric tonnes.

Answer:

1 tonne = 1,000 kg

7,500 ÷ 1,000 = 7.5 tonnes

Result: 7.5 tonnes.

6. Calculate load percentage of crane capacity

Question: A crane's rated capacity for the specified configuration and radius is 20 tonnes. The total planned load is 12 tonnes. Calculate the percentage of rated capacity.

Answer:

Percentage = (Load ÷ Rated Capacity) × 100

Percentage = (12 ÷ 20) × 100 = 60%

Result: 60% of rated capacity.

This percentage does not by itself establish that a lift is safe. Verify the exact load chart, radius, configuration, deductions, ground conditions and approved lift plan.

7. Calculate the load weight from volume

Question: A material occupies 2.5 m³ and has a density of 800 kg/m³. Calculate its mass.

Answer:

Mass = Volume × Density

Mass = 2.5 × 800 = 2,000 kg

Result: 2,000 kg or 2 tonnes.

8. Calculate the force from a mass

Question: Calculate the approximate weight force of a 500 kg object using g = 9.81 m/s².

Answer:

Force = Mass × Gravitational acceleration

F = 500 × 9.81 = 4,905 N

4,905 N = 4.905 kN

Result: approximately 4.905 kN.

9. Calculate the volume of a cylindrical load

Question: A solid cylinder has a radius of 0.5 m and a height of 2 m. Calculate its volume. Use π = 3.1416.

Answer:

Volume = π × radius² × height

V = 3.1416 × 0.5² × 2

V = 1.5708 m³

Result: approximately 1.571 m³.

10. Calculate the mass of the cylinder

Question: Using the cylinder volume from Question 9 and a density of 7,850 kg/m³, calculate its mass.

Answer:

Mass = Volume × Density

Mass = 1.5708 × 7,850 ≈ 12,330 kg

Result: approximately 12.33 tonnes.

This assumes a solid steel cylinder and excludes any hollow space, attachments or other deductions.

Important safety reminder

These examples use simplified assumptions for learning. Real sling forces can vary with unequal load sharing, sling geometry, hitch type, centre of gravity and dynamic effects. Use certified equipment within its rated limits and follow the approved lift plan and site rules.
