Questions & Answers

Read each question, think about your answer, then open the answer below it.

These are original practice study notes, not official TÜV examination questions. Always follow current manufacturer instructions, applicable standards, your approved lift plan and site procedures.

1. What does WLL mean?
Answer: Working Load Limit. It is the manufacturer's rated limit for a specified equipment configuration and use.
2. What should you check before using a sling?
Answer: Check its condition, identification tag, rated capacity, suitability, connections and any damage or defects.
3. What should you do if a sling's identification tag is missing?
Answer: Remove it from service until its identity and capacity can be verified through the approved process. Never guess its WLL.
4. What is the centre of gravity (COG)?
Answer: The point through which the resultant weight of an object can be considered to act.
5. Why is the centre of gravity important?
Answer: It helps determine load balance, stability, lifting-point suitability and how the load may move when lifted.
6. What happens when sling legs become shallower?
Answer: Sling-leg tension generally increases as the angle measured from horizontal decreases. The approved lift plan and equipment ratings must be checked.
7. What should you do if you find a damaged shackle?
Answer: Stop using it, prevent further use and report it for assessment under the site's inspection procedure.
8. Why must people stay clear of suspended loads?
Answer: A load can fall, swing or shift and cause serious or fatal injuries. Keep people outside the designated hazard area.
9. What should the operator do if a signal is unclear?
Answer: Stop the movement safely and re-establish clear communication before continuing.
10. What should happen if an emergency stop signal is given?
Answer: The operator must stop the movement safely and follow the site's procedure.
11. Why must ground conditions be checked before crane setup?
Answer: Weak ground, voids or underground services can cause settlement, equipment instability or overturning.
12. What is the purpose of a crane load chart?
Answer: It specifies the crane's rated lifting capacity for defined configurations and operating conditions.
13. What should you do if a load starts tilting unexpectedly?
Answer: Stop the lift, lower the load safely if possible, and reassess the centre of gravity, rigging arrangement and lifting points.
14. Why should sharp edges be protected?
Answer: Sharp edges can cut or damage slings. Use suitable edge protection according to the sling manufacturer's guidance.
15. What should you do if the lift conditions change from the plan?
Answer: Stop and reassess the risks. Revise and obtain the required approval for the lift plan before continuing.
16. What is a choker hitch?
Answer: A sling arrangement in which the sling passes around the load and through itself or its designed fitting to form a choke. Use the applicable rated capacity for that hitch.
17. Why should slings not be knotted or twisted?
Answer: Knots and harmful twists can damage the sling and change how forces are carried.
18. What is a tag line used for?
Answer: Where appropriate in the lift plan, it helps control load rotation or movement from a safe position. It must not create entanglement or other hazards.
19. What should you do if a load snags during lifting?
Answer: Stop the movement and reassess. Never force a snagged load by applying extra crane force or sudden movement.
20. Why is a pre-lift toolbox talk important?
Answer: It confirms the method, hazards, controls, team roles, signals and actions required if conditions become unsafe.
