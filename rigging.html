Lifting Accessories Reference

The information is intended for study and reference. The manufacturer's instructions, certification, applicable Saudi requirements, project specifications and approved lifting procedure take precedence.

1. Webbing Slings

Synthetic webbing slings are flexible lifting slings commonly used for lifting loads where surface protection is important.

Common Types

  • Flat webbing sling
  • Eye-and-eye sling
  • Endless sling
  • Single-layer and multi-layer webbing
  • Different eye configurations

Common Hitch Arrangements

  • Vertical / straight lift
  • Choker hitch
  • Basket hitch
  • Multiple-leg arrangements

Common Standard Reference

EN 1492-1 is commonly associated with flat woven webbing slings made from man-made fibres. ASME B30.9 also addresses synthetic webbing slings.

Identification

  • Manufacturer
  • Rated capacity / WLL
  • Material
  • Length
  • Identification number where applicable
  • Applicable hitch capacities

Colour Identification

Colour Typical EN Capacity Identification
Violet 1 tonne
Green 2 tonnes
Yellow 3 tonnes
Grey 4 tonnes
Red 5 tonnes
Brown 6 tonnes
Blue 8 tonnes
Orange 10 tonnes and above
Important: Colour must not be used as the only method of determining sling capacity. Always read the manufacturer's identification label and WLL.

Inspection

  • Check identification label is present and legible.
  • Check cuts, tears and holes.
  • Check abrasion and excessive wear.
  • Check heat or chemical damage.
  • Check stitching and eyes.
  • Check contamination that may damage the fibres.
  • Check protective sleeves and edge protection.

2. Round Slings

Round slings are synthetic fibre lifting slings with a continuous load-bearing core protected by an outer cover.

Common Standard

EN 1492-2 covers roundslings made from man-made fibres. ASME B30.9 also includes synthetic fibre rope and covered polyester slings within its scope.

Inspection

  • Check outer cover for cuts and abrasion.
  • Check for exposed load-bearing core.
  • Check heat damage.
  • Check chemical damage.
  • Check stitching and identification.
  • Check WLL marking.
If the load-bearing core is damaged or exposed, remove the sling from service and have it assessed according to the applicable procedure.

4. Chain Slings

Alloy steel chain slings are used for demanding lifting applications and can be configured as single-, two-, three- or four-leg assemblies.

Common Components

  • Master link
  • Connecting link
  • Chain leg
  • Shortening hook
  • Grab hook where approved
  • Clevis hook

Grades

Common industrial alloy chain lifting grades include Grade 8, Grade 10 and Grade 12. The actual WLL must always be taken from the manufacturer's identification and applicable standard.

Inspection

  • Check chain links for deformation.
  • Check for cracks and gouges.
  • Check excessive wear.
  • Check corrosion.
  • Check stretched or elongated links.
  • Check hooks and latches.
  • Check identification and WLL.

5. Shackles

Shackles connect lifting slings, hooks, lifting points and other components.

Bow / Anchor Shackle

A bow shackle has a wider rounded body. It is useful where multiple sling legs or wider connections must be accommodated.

D / Chain Shackle

A D shackle has a narrower body and is commonly used for more direct in-line loading.

Feature Bow Shackle D Shackle
Body Wide / rounded Narrow / D-shaped
Typical use Multiple or wider connections More direct in-line connection
Common application Multi-leg sling connections Single-leg or direct connections

Types

  • Screw-pin shackle
  • Safety / bolt-type shackle
  • Bow shackle
  • D shackle

Common Standard Reference

ASME B30.26 covers rigging hardware, including detachable rigging hardware such as shackles.

Inspection

  • Check WLL marking.
  • Check body deformation.
  • Check pin condition.
  • Check threads.
  • Check cracks and corrosion.
  • Check excessive wear.
  • Check correct pin and body combination.
Never substitute a bolt, ordinary steel bar or unapproved pin for the manufacturer's shackle pin.

6. Lifting Hooks

Types

  • Eye hook
  • Clevis hook
  • Swivel hook
  • Self-locking hook
  • Foundry / special-purpose hook

Common Standard

ASME B30.10 covers hooks used with lifting equipment.

Inspection

  • Check hook identification and WLL.
  • Check hook throat opening.
  • Check deformation.
  • Check cracks and gouges.
  • Check excessive wear.
  • Check latch operation where fitted.
  • Check swivel and bearing condition where applicable.
A hook with significant deformation, cracking or other unacceptable damage must not remain in service.

8. Lifting Beams & Spreader Beams

Lifting Beam

A lifting beam generally carries load between lower lifting points and the crane connection. Its structural design can involve significant bending.

Spreader Beam

A spreader beam is generally used to distribute lifting forces and maintain separation between lifting points. Depending on design, compression and tension effects are important.

Inspection

  • Check identification and rated load.
  • Check structural members.
  • Check welds.
  • Check lifting lugs and padeyes.
  • Check pins and connections.
  • Check deformation and corrosion.
  • Check certification and inspection status.

ASME B30.20 addresses below-the-hook lifting devices. Structural design requirements may also involve ASME BTH-1 or other applicable design standards.

10. Eyebolts & Lifting Points

Types

  • Plain eye bolt
  • Shoulder eye bolt
  • Swivel lifting point
  • Engineered lifting point

Important Difference

Plain and shoulder eye bolts have different loading limitations. A lifting point designed for angular loading must not be replaced by a standard eye bolt without verifying the manufacturer's requirements.

Inspection

  • Check threads.
  • Check deformation.
  • Check cracks.
  • Check corrosion.
  • Check seating.
  • Check manufacturer's marking and WLL.

16. Inspection & Rejection

Inspection requirements depend on the equipment, applicable standard, manufacturer instructions, site procedure and statutory requirements.

Pre-Use Inspection

  • Confirm identification.
  • Confirm WLL/rated capacity is visible.
  • Check certification/inspection status where required.
  • Check for visible damage.
  • Check connections and pins.
  • Check correct configuration.
  • Check compatibility with the load.

Common Reasons for Removal from Service

  • Cracks
  • Severe deformation
  • Excessive wear
  • Heat damage
  • Severe corrosion
  • Unapproved modification
  • Missing or illegible identification
  • Damaged stitching or load-bearing fibres
  • Damaged chain or wire rope
  • Damaged hooks, pins or connecting components
Remove from service: Equipment showing damage that could affect its safe use should be removed from service and assessed according to the applicable manufacturer, inspection and site procedure.
