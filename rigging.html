Lifting & Rigging Safety Reference

Lifting operations involve significant hazards. Effective planning, competent personnel, suitable equipment, appropriate controls and clear communication are essential.

This page is a study and reference resource and does not replace site-specific risk assessments, approved lift plans, regulations, manufacturer instructions or competent-person requirements.

1. Suspended Load Hazards

Suspended Loads

Personnel should not position themselves beneath suspended loads. Loads must be controlled and the lifting area appropriately managed.

Dropped Objects

Dropped loads, rigging components or unsecured objects can cause serious injury or property damage.

  • Use suitable lifting equipment
  • Inspect equipment
  • Secure the load appropriately
  • Establish an exclusion zone
  • Keep unauthorized personnel away

Load Swing

Loads can swing because of wind, acceleration, deceleration, inadequate control or incorrect lifting arrangements.

2. Rigging Hazards

Damaged Slings

Cuts, abrasion, broken wires, deformation, heat damage or chemical damage can compromise lifting equipment.

Damaged Shackles

Shackles should be checked for deformation, excessive wear, damage, incorrect components and identification.

Incorrect Sling Configuration

The lifting arrangement must be suitable for the load, equipment, connection points and planned configuration.

Sharp Edges

Sharp load edges can damage synthetic slings and wire rope. Appropriate protection should be selected where required.

Incorrect Sling Angles

Sling geometry affects leg forces and must be considered during lifting planning and calculations.

3. Crane Hazards

Crane Overturning

Crane stability can be affected by load radius, configuration, ground conditions, wind and incorrect setup.

Ground Failure

Weak or unsuitable ground can cause settlement or loss of stability. Ground conditions should be assessed as part of lift planning.

Outrigger Failure

Outriggers and supporting arrangements must be correctly configured and supported according to the crane manufacturer's requirements.

Power Lines

Overhead electrical lines create a potentially fatal electrical hazard. Required clearances and controls must be established before lifting operations.

Crane Collision

Crane structures, loads and nearby equipment can collide if clearances and movement paths are not properly controlled.

4. Load Hazards

Overloading

A crane or lifting accessory must not be loaded beyond its applicable rated capacity or manufacturer's specified limits.

Unknown Load Weight

Load weight should be established or reliably determined before planning the lift.

Unbalanced Load

Loads with an unknown or offset center of gravity can tilt or shift when lifted.

Uncontrolled Load Movement

Loads may move unexpectedly because of wind, snagging, incorrect rigging or inadequate control.

5. Personnel Hazards

Crush and Pinch Points

Hands, feet and body parts can be trapped between the load, rigging equipment and surrounding structures.

Caught-Between Hazards

Personnel should remain outside areas where moving loads or equipment could trap or crush them.

Unauthorized Personnel

Only personnel required for the lifting operation should enter controlled lifting areas.

6. Environmental Hazards

Wind

Wind can affect crane stability and cause large or suspended loads to behave unpredictably.

Rain and Poor Visibility

Poor weather and visibility can affect communication, footing, inspection and load control.

Lightning

Lightning and severe weather may require lifting operations to be suspended according to site procedures and manufacturer requirements.

Extreme Temperatures

Temperature can affect equipment, personnel and some materials. Manufacturer and site requirements should be followed.

7. Site Hazards

Excavations

Crane setup near excavations requires careful consideration of ground stability and site engineering requirements.

Traffic

Vehicle and pedestrian traffic can create collision and exclusion-zone hazards around lifting operations.

Structures

Buildings, pipe racks, scaffolding and other structures can restrict crane and load movement.

Poor Lighting

Insufficient lighting can reduce visibility of the load, rigging, signals and surrounding hazards.

8. Planning & Risk Controls

Lift Plan

A suitable lift plan should identify the load, crane, rigging, lifting arrangement, work area, hazards, controls, communication and required personnel.

Risk Assessment

Hazards should be identified and appropriate controls established before the lifting operation.

Method Statement

The method statement should describe the planned work sequence, responsibilities, controls and communication arrangements.

Pre-Lift Meeting

The lifting team should understand the plan, responsibilities, communication arrangements and stop-work requirements before the lift.

9. Communication

Hand Signals

Approved crane hand signals should be used by designated personnel where applicable.

Radio Communication

Radio communication should be clear, agreed and tested before operations when radios are required.

One Designated Signaller

The lifting team should establish clear authority for signals to avoid conflicting instructions.

10. Inspection & Equipment Control

Pre-Use Inspection

  • Check identification
  • Check rated capacity information
  • Check hooks and latches
  • Check shackles and pins
  • Check slings
  • Check wire ropes
  • Check lifting points
  • Check crane safety devices

Damaged Equipment

Equipment that is damaged, unidentified or suspected to be unsuitable should be removed from service or controlled according to applicable procedures until properly assessed.

11. Personal Protective Equipment

PPE

  • Safety helmet
  • Safety footwear
  • Suitable gloves
  • Eye protection
  • High-visibility clothing
  • Hearing protection where required
  • Additional PPE based on the task risk assessment

12. Emergency Planning

Emergency Response

Lifting operations should have appropriate emergency arrangements covering foreseeable incidents such as dropped loads, equipment failure, injury, fire, severe weather or loss of communication.

Stop Work

Personnel should have a clear means to stop the operation when an unsafe condition develops or when the lift no longer matches the approved plan.

13. Lifting Safety Checklist

Pre-Lift Checklist

  • Lift plan reviewed
  • Risk assessment completed
  • Load weight established
  • Center of gravity considered
  • Correct crane selected
  • Load chart verified
  • Correct lifting accessories selected
  • Accessories inspected
  • Crane inspected
  • Ground conditions assessed
  • Outriggers/support arrangements verified
  • Exclusion zone established
  • Communication method agreed
  • Weather conditions checked
  • Personnel briefed
  • Emergency arrangements understood

Important Safety Notice

This website is intended for education and study purposes only. It does not replace an approved lift plan, task-specific risk assessment, engineering assessment, manufacturer instructions, applicable laws, regulations, standards or competent-person verification.

Actual lifting operations must be planned and controlled by appropriately competent personnel using equipment suitable for the specific task and conditions.
